# From captured material to understanding

A later research design identified an important limitation: collecting and summarizing more material does not ensure that the person using the system learns from it.

That design proposed a loop with four different jobs:

1. **Explore:** gather relevant perspectives around an open question, including credible disagreement.
2. **Synthesize:** connect claims, supporting sources, recurring ideas and contradictions.
3. **Generalize:** propose a transferable principle, including its limits and strongest counterargument.
4. **Revisit:** ask the person to explain or challenge the principle, then review it when new evidence arrives.

The earlier template's selective atomic notes and the later principle proposal share an aim: retain useful ideas rather than create a smaller summary of every captured source. The later design adds inquiry, counterarguments and deliberate resurfacing.

## Human understanding remains the purpose

The proposed autonomy rule is that an agent drafts and the person decides what to adopt. Corpus agreement and personal confidence are separate. A system should not silently change someone's stated beliefs because a cluster of sources repeats the same claim.

Open questions can organize the research. New contradictory evidence can reopen an apparently settled question. Source diversity needs qualitative review too; counting sources or contradictions does not establish independence, truth or healthy disagreement by itself.

## Status of this direction

This is a recovered design direction, not a running learning product in this repository. The broader sources contain claim-processing and insight components. The reviewed research plan proposed additional principle promotion, sparring, resurfacing and calibration work. This release does not claim those loops operated successfully or improved learning outcomes.

The public template retains the smaller prerequisite: an idea with an explicit source and a visible qualification. Expanding it should begin with a real question and evidence of usefulness, rather than an automated quota of notes.
